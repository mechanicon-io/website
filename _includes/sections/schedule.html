16:40 – 17:00
PolyKybd: Displays In Your Keycaps by Thomas Pollak (thpoll)
description

In 2020 I looked at the small displays on my desk and asked: would one fit into a keycap? Answering that question took six years.

The first displays almost fit. The next ones fit, but their cables were too short, so I soldered an extension onto every single one. I changed the microcontroller three times, once because of the chip shortage. I learned KiCad for this project and threw away my first PCB layout. I went from one key to 8, to 20, to a full split keyboard. Then the displays glitched, and I had to find out why one chip could not drive 36 of them. I wrote my own font converter to support any language, and I generate the case from code so it follows every PCB change.

Then came the software. The keyboard has to know which program is active and show the right shortcuts, on Windows, Linux and macOS, over a slow connection. That part stayed unfinished for a long time. The last mile finally happened with the help of AI. More on that in the talk.

The result is the PolyKybd Split72, a keyboard where every key tells you what it does.
